# Deck narrative structures

Pick the closest match to the user's purpose, then adapt. These are starting skeletons, not rigid templates — cut or merge slides to fit the actual content.

## Investor pitch deck
1. Title (company, one-line tagline)
2. Problem (who hurts, how badly, today)
3. Solution (what you do, in one sentence + visual)
4. Why now (market timing/trend)
5. Market size (TAM/SAM/SOM)
6. Product (screenshots/demo narrative)
7. Business model (how you make money)
8. Traction (the strongest numbers you have — growth, revenue, logos)
9. Competition/landscape (positioning, not a feature checklist)
10. Go-to-market
11. Team (why you're the ones to win this)
12. Financials/projections
13. The ask (how much, what for)

## Quarterly Business Review (QBR) / internal update
1. Title + period covered
2. TL;DR — 3 headline takeaways (good, bad, and what changed)
3. Key metrics vs. target (dashboard-style)
4. What went well (with the "why," not just the number)
5. What went wrong / risks (own it, don't bury it)
6. Deep dive on 1–2 most important metrics
7. What's changing next period
8. Asks / blockers / decisions needed

## Sales / client-facing deck
1. Title (client name if personalised)
2. Their problem, in their words/context (shows you listened)
3. Your solution mapped directly to that problem
4. Proof — case studies, logos, numbers (not generic feature lists)
5. How it works / what implementation looks like
6. Pricing/packages (if appropriate to include)
7. Why us vs. alternatives
8. Clear next step with a date

## Project status update
1. Title + project name + date
2. Status at a glance (on track / at risk / blocked — be honest, use color not just words)
3. What shipped since last update
4. What's in progress
5. Risks/blockers with owners
6. Next milestones + dates
7. Decisions needed from this audience

## Board report
1. Title + period
2. Executive summary (headline numbers + narrative in plain language)
3. Financial performance vs. plan
4. Operational highlights
5. Risks and mitigations
6. Strategic initiatives update
7. Asks/approvals needed
8. Appendix (detailed tables — keep OUT of the main flow)

## Conference talk / thought leadership
1. Title + hook (a question, surprising stat, or bold claim)
2. Why this matters to this audience specifically
3. 2–4 main points, each with one supporting story/data point
4. A memorable framework or takeaway they can repeat to others
5. Call to action / where to learn more

---

## General pacing rule
Roughly 1 slide per minute of spoken time for a live talk; denser for a deck that will be read standalone (async). If the user says "this is for reading, not presenting," allow more text density per slide but still avoid raw data dumps — use well-labeled visuals.
